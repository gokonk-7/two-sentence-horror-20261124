# The story

> The new tenant in 4B leaves a different shoe outside my door every morning, always my size, always the left one. Today there were two, and the right one was still warm.

168 characters, exactly two sentences.

## Why this one

Judged against the published brief, in the brief's own order:

1. **Genuinely frightening, exactly two sentences.** A stranger has been
   leaving shoes at the narrator's door. That is already wrong; the text
   stays inside two sentences and the 400-character cap.
2. **The second sentence turns the first.** On a first read the shoes are
   a neighbour's odd habit. On the second read they were a countdown: a
   left shoe every morning, always the narrator's size, until today the
   pair is complete — and the right one is still warm, so the person who
   has been leaving them is right outside the door, barefoot, right now.
   The turn lands on two words, "still warm": warmth is recent, and
   recent means present.
3. **Implied, not explained.** No monster is named, no one is harmed on
   the page, nothing is spelled out. The reader supplies the worst of it.
4. **Precise and concrete.** Every detail earns its place: "4B" places
   it, "my size" makes it personal, "always the left one" sets the
   pattern, "still warm" breaks it.
5. **Original.** The scare is not a ghost, a mirror, a dead relative
   calling, or a child with an imaginary friend. It is a shoe.

## Variants considered and set aside

- *Voicemail (223 chars):* "My voicemail has been full for three years,
  ever since my mother died mid-sentence on the last message she ever
  left me. This morning it said I had one new message, and the playback
  was my own voice, finishing her sentence." Strong turn, but the dead
  relative's last message is a familiar horror beat, it explains more
  than it implies, and it is longer.
- *Dog and chair (160 chars):* "I adopted the dog from the shelter
  because he would not stop staring at the empty chair in the corner of
  my living room. He stopped staring the day I sat in it." Good turn,
  but the logic is murkier on a second read — the chair reading is
  ambiguous in a way that blurs rather than sharpens.
- *Imaginary friend (189 chars):* "My daughter's imaginary friend
  stopped visiting the day she turned seven, and she cried for a week.
  She is twenty now, and last night she asked me why I still set a
  place for him at dinner." A clean turn, but the imaginary friend is
  one of the most familiar twists in the genre.

The shoe story won on originality and on the sharpest second-sentence
turn, and at 168 characters it is also the smallest of the finalists.
